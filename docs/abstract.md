# Abstract

Depression is among the most prevalent and disabling mental health disorders worldwide, yet its diagnosis remains largely subjective and prone to bias, creating a pressing need for objective, automated screening tools. This project, **TriDep**, presents a tri-modal deep learning framework for the binary classification of depression from clinical interview recordings, integrating three behavioural modalities: text, audio, and video. The interview transcript is encoded using a pre-trained Sentence-BERT model, the patient's speech using a pre-trained Wav2Vec2 model, and the patient's facial behaviour using Facial Action Units extracted with OpenFace. The three representations are combined through a model-level fusion architecture that processes each modality independently before a shared network produces the final classification.

The system was developed on the DAIC-WOZ clinical interview dataset (189 subjects) and evaluated through two iterations. A **Baseline approach** used Leave-One-Subject-Out Cross-Validation with a simple dense fusion network and binary cross-entropy loss, achieving an accuracy of approximately 70% and a depressed-class F1 score of approximately 0.46 under strict subject-independent evaluation. Building on this, an **Improved approach** introduced 5-Fold Stratified Cross-Validation, a regularised BatchNorm fusion network, Focal Loss to address class imbalance, SMOTE oversampling on training folds, and an optimal Youden-J decision threshold. Full comparative metrics for both approaches, including unimodal ablations, confusion matrices, and ROC curves, are provided in `results/figures/`.

A key finding across both iterations is that the text modality consistently carries the strongest individual signal, while naive fusion of weaker audio and video modalities does not automatically improve performance — a result that highlights the conditional nature of multimodal fusion benefits. The project demonstrates that automated multimodal depression screening is feasible, and that rigorous, leakage-free evaluation is essential for producing honest, generalisable results on small clinical datasets. This system is presented as a research-grade screening-support prototype and is not intended for clinical diagnostic use.
